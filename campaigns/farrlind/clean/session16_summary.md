Session Summary: Session 16

Physical Session Date: 2026-01-16
In-Game Date: 1832 AS Namal 12, 1832 AS Namal 13

The session began on Remembrance Day, where the party shared stories of those they had lost. Mikani spoke of those who perished in the Celestial Isles, while Corvinas remembered his village and the struggles he faced. Roon recalled his shield, and Brigit thought back to her alcohol.

As the night wore on, the party's mood shifted, and I began singing "The Keeper of the Quiet Key." However, my performance was marred by my growing maudlin state, and I eventually broke down.

Roon took me upstairs without a word, and Mikani approached the mysterious satchel that had been left behind. She described it as old but well-cared-for, with deliberate sigils that seemed to interrupt rather than repel.

Mikani found the central rune next, which felt older than spellwork. Gildas then cast his spell, but it eventually fractured and dissolved into absences. He was unable to analyze the magic further, and Brigit watched the entire time.

Corvinas did not join us upstairs, instead remaining in the tavern below. When I woke later, no one asked me to explain my actions or emotions.

The party's questions turned to the tasks that remain unanswered, including what faith casts Silence so readily. Mikani spoke of unease and Iron Paw, and we learned that the eastern coast remembers conquest even when it claims forgetfulness.

We then went to the Namaloan temple, where Iron Paw stood waiting. He summoned a Wasterlich and three fish-things, which attacked us. Mikani responded with ice and storm, while Gildas used fire to cut through the battlefield. Corvinas fell beneath the fish-things, but Roon and I managed to keep hope alive.

Iron Paw then summoned a Dracolich, which missed Corvinas by chance. Mikani returned with healing, and Gildas continued to use his magic. Brigit was struck by hellfire, but Corvinas ended it with divine force. I bent the Wasterlich's will, and we eventually defeated Iron Paw.

In the aftermath, I realized that I do not fear death, but rather being forgotten incorrectly. The party's thoughts on their actions are unclear, but I know that I can never allow myself to lose control again.
