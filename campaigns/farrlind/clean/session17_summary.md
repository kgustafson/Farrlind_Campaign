Title: After Iron Paw

We searched the remains of Iron Paw with reverence and caution. Gildas prepared his detection spell to read the echoes clinging to the fallen warlock's tools. Roon claimed the great sword with necrotic hunger, while Mikani took an abjuration ring. We found a survivor, Erling Rostad, who spoke of primordial forces and the Cataclysm as over-release. Iron Paw viewed the Celestial Isles as an abomination.

We asked about water-breathing magic for our eventual descent toward Catur and were told about Whisper Root. The apothecary proved cooperative, and we obtained twenty potions of borrowed gills and temporary courage. Our route was set: Balrog first, dwarven depths, then Catur below the sea.

We traveled to the Crossroads Festival, where we encountered revelry without illusion or glamour. We accepted the festival's joy, with some of my best work in ballads, jigs, and tavern filth. Mikani attempted harmony, nearly summoning the grave. I composed a rescue jig on the spot, which saved her dignity.

We had uneventful travel, but Gildas asked about the old tale that will not leave me: The Prince of True Speech. He listened like a man hearing a map described for the first time and realizing he has already walked it.

We found the dwarven opening to Balrog and entered the city, where we encountered a sleeping guard at a crossroads. We woke him in his own tongue and asked about ale and magic. He pointed us correctly on both counts.

Inside Balrog, we found a black obelisk tower dominating the city like a held breath. Our inn greeted us with severed-digit humor and generous hostility. I struck a bargain with the barkeep to raise his take by a quarter and drink free. A brawl began, but a larger dwarf ended it efficiently without discussion.

I sang of the Wells, the Cataclysm, and power released without restraint. One gem appraiser, Forgebottom, faltered at the refrain and ruined a stone. Corvinas overheard infernal speech in the crowd: You have them; Meet me at the tower; We begin then. The tower again, always towers.

A figure fell from the highest window of the black tower, beginning the long argument with gravity. I do not think it was an accident.
